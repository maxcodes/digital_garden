I was already using AI at work when I started
[cooking with my mom using Cooking with Claude](https://github.com/maxcodes/cooking-with-claude/tree/main/examples/christmas-2025).
That was the beginning of using it to improve my life outside work. Since
then, the two have kept feeding into each other: things I learn at work find
their way home, and things I try at home change how I use AI at work.

Cooking gave me somewhere to start. We worked on menus, adjusted portions,
and put together shopping lists. Recipes and preferences stayed in the
project, so we could build on them the next time we cooked.

That grew into Rita, my personal assistant, who runs on
[OpenClaw](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw). She has access to tools and
files on my computer, and we talk through WhatsApp. Other people in my life
can talk to her there too.

Someone cooking at home can ask her what to make, tell her an ingredient is
missing, or ask for help with a step in a recipe. When there was no pesto for
a pasta dish, she adapted it to garlic and lemon. When a recipe called for
peeled peppers, she explained how to roast and steam them to loosen the skin.
Those conversations happen directly with her.

She also helps check what actually arrived in a grocery delivery, clarify
availability with shops, and pass along household instructions. People can
ask follow-up questions and correct her as they go. I can give her a task
that involves a conversation with someone else, then get the answer back.

## Teaching it how you want things done

Across both work and home, I've had to get better at explaining what I want.
Give AI the information it needs. Show it a good example. When something is
wrong, explain why. When something works, say "always do it like that" and
have it save the instruction for next time.

With Rita, those instructions live in files she reads. They include food
preferences, how to handle recipe requests, and when to check with me. They
change as we use her and discover what needs adjusting.

At Recurrente, the payments company where I build software, I have Claude
Code and Codex review each other's work and send findings back for
corrections. The same questions matter at home: did it understand the
request, does the result work for the person using it, and what should it
keep for next time?

A useful place to start is something you keep repeating. The third time you
do it, try handing it over. Give yourself time to work through the first
attempt and check the result. Keep the instructions that made it useful.

## Bringing this to WhatsApp

At Recurrente, we're building [Cliq](https://www.recurrente.com/cliq), a
WhatsApp assistant you can use without setting up something like Rita
yourself. It will remember preferences you ask it to keep and help with
paying bills, buying things at the pharmacy, and booking appointments. It
will ask for your approval before moving money.
