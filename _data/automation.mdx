Treat AI like someone you're training to take tasks off your hands. Hand it
what you keep repeating, push it to think harder, show it good work, have it
grade the result, and make it keep what works.

## From cooking with my mom to a personal assistant

My first real "aha" moment with [Claude Code](https://claude.ai/code) was
[cooking with my
mom](https://github.com/maxcodes/cooking-with-claude/tree/main/examples/christmas-2025).
We planned two four-course meals and a turkey. It read our recipes, adjusted
portions, and made shopping lists. It also saved our preferences, so the next
meal could start from what we'd taught it.

I wanted that memory for everything else at home, so I set up Rita, a personal
assistant that remembers my household's preferences. She runs on
[OpenClaw](https://github.com/openclaw/openclaw), which connects AI to tools
and files on my computer. I talk to her through WhatsApp, and she helps Mirna,
who cooks for us, with recipes and shopping lists.

What worked with both comes down to five rules.

## Five rules

### 1. The third time, hand the task to AI

Pay attention to what you do each day. **The third time you do something, ask
AI to do it for you.** Give it the task, the information you normally use, and
a description of the finished result.

### 2. Tell it to "use your brain"

When its answer feels shallow or it gets stuck on its first approach, tell it
**"use your brain."** Push it to think harder and try a different angle.

### 3. Show it your standard

Hold it to a high standard. Give it examples of good work and explain what
makes them good. **When its attempt falls short, say why** and ask it to
revise.

### 4. Make it grade its work until it scores 9.5

Ask it to **grade its work out of 10 and keep improving until it reaches
9.5**. Have it explain what's missing, fix those gaps, and check the result
against your original request before grading again.

For a stronger review, use a separate session that didn't do the work. Give
that reviewer your request, examples, and the finished result. At Recurrente,
the payments company where I build software, I have Claude Code and Codex
review each other's work and send the findings back for corrections.

### 5. Tell it "always do it like that"

When it finally gets the work right, say **"always do it like that"**. Ask it
to save that as an instruction it reads every time, the way it saved our
cooking preferences.

## Cliq: an assistant like Rita, without the setup

At Recurrente, we're building [Cliq](https://www.recurrente.com/cliq): a
WhatsApp assistant like Rita that you don't have to set up yourself. It will
remember the preferences you ask it to keep, so you can hand it recurring
tasks and teach it how you like them done: paying bills, buying things at the
pharmacy, booking appointments. It will always ask for your approval before
moving money.
